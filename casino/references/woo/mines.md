## Coverage note

The Wizard has a dedicated page for Mines at `wizardofodds.com/games/mines/` — this is his
Stake-Originals-style Mines analysis (5x5 grid, 1-24 mines, cash-out-anytime). Note two caveats:

1. On the page he says "As far as I know, the game can be played exclusively at BetFury" — i.e.
   the pay table he analyzes is the BetFury version, which returns roughly 95%. Stake's own
   Mines uses a 99%-RTP multiplier schedule (fair multiplier x 0.99); the Wizard analyzes that
   99% structure on his separate Minesweeper (Encrypted Version) page (see below). There is no
   page at /games/mines-stake/ or /games/stake-mines/ (both 404).
2. The game mechanics are identical to Stake Mines; only the multiplier schedule (and hence RTP)
   differs between operators.

## Rules (as stated by the Wizard)

- Played on a 5x5 grid (25 cells).
- Player bets and chooses the number of mines hidden in the grid, from 1 to 24. All non-mine
  cells hold diamonds.
- Player picks cells, trying to avoid mines. Each diamond found (usually) increases the win.
- After each successful pick the player may cash out or continue, as long as diamonds remain.
- All wins are quoted on a "for one" basis (the multiplier includes return of stake).

## Headline house edge / RTP figures (Wizard's summary, verbatim in substance)

- "In cases of 2 to 24 mines, the expected return is always close to 95%" — i.e. house edge ~5%
  on this (BetFury) pay table.
- "In general, the expected return for the first pick is close to 95% and for all subsequent
  picks is close to 100%." (The ~5% edge is taken on the first pick; continuing after that is
  roughly a fair gamble at these pays.)
- Exception — one mine: "the expected return is 96% for one pick, close to 95% for 24 picks,
  and much less, as low as 41% for 2 to 23 picks." (His own table row for 1 mine / 1 pick shows
  pays 1.03, P(win) 0.96, return 0.9888; the 41% trough is at 1 mine / 21 picks, return
  0.358400. The 96%-for-one-pick prose and the 98.88% table row do not quite agree — both are
  captured here verbatim; the detailed table is presumably authoritative.)
- Wizard's advice: "either choose at least two mines and play as long as [you] like, or one
  mine and either stop at one diamond or go all the way to all 24."

## Methodology notes

- Probability of surviving n picks with m mines is the hypergeometric product
  prod_{i=0..n-1} (25-m-i)/(25-i); return = pays x P(win). His analysis table lists, for every
  (mines, picks) combination: the pay ("for one"), probability of winning, and expected return.
- He evaluates the game per final stopping point (pick n and cash out), not per individual pick;
  the per-pick observation (first pick ~95%, later picks ~100%) follows from ratios of adjacent
  rows.
- The Wizard publishes NO standard deviation or variance figures for Mines — neither on the
  Mines page nor on the Minesweeper (Encrypted Version) page. Nothing to capture on that front.
- Apparent typos/outliers in his table (captured as published): 15 mines / 1 pick shows pays
  2.51 and return 1.004000 (>100%; the fair 0.95 pay would be 2.375, and his own 13-18-mine pay
  table also lists 2.51); 16 mines / 5 picks shows pays 458 and return 1.086166 (>100%; the
  5-mines/16-picks symmetric cell pays 400.58, which would return ~0.949992). Treat those two
  rows with suspicion.

## Supplementary: Minesweeper (Encrypted Version)

Source: https://wizardofodds.com/games/minesweeper-cryptocurrency/

Same 5x5, 1-24 mines game as offered by crypto casinos (analyzed version: CryptoGames), with the
99%-RTP multiplier structure that Stake-style originals use:

- Multiplier after each pick follows stake x (25/(25-m)) x ... adjusted by a 0.99 factor:
  e.g. with 3 mines, stake x (25/22) x (24/21) x (23/20) x ... x 0.99.
- RTP: 99% on the first pick, 100% on each subsequent pick; overall return per resolved bet 99%
  ("at least for the version at CryptoGames"). House edge 1%.
- Provably-fair methodology: mine locations predetermined by SHA-512 hash of combined
  server/client seeds; he supplies PHP verification code. He notes a tiny positional bias in the
  1-mine/1-pick case: squares 21-24 return 99.00% vs 98.97% for squares 0-20.
- No standard deviation / variance figures published on this page either.
